# Part One: Claim Register

Every load-bearing factual claim in Part One, with its source and status. This is
the document that catches assertions that entered from a search snippet three
drafts ago and were never checked.

**Status key.** **P** = primary source read directly. **S** = scholarly secondary
source read directly. **R** = reported at one or more removes. **I** = my own
inference or interpretation, not a sourced fact.

**Updated 2 August 2026 after acquiring Marsh 1866** (`marsh-1866-temperance-recollections.txt`).
Four claims moved from "at a remove" to "primary, read at source": the Beecher letter of
21 January 1845, now quoted in full rather than paraphrased through Maxwell; the statement
that the movement's fruits were gathered into new organisations, which is the absorption
thesis in a participant's own words; the Connecticut convention and the Washingtonians'
change of position on prohibition; and Marsh's exclusion from the Sons of Temperance for not
being a reformed man. One claim was added to Chapter 1 as a *conflict* rather than a
confirmation: Marsh's founding account names Elder Knapp where Maxwell names Matthew Hale
Smith, and has four delegates where Maxwell has one.

**The pattern is clear and it is the pattern the acquisition plan predicts.** The
three chapters resting on documents read at source are solid. The three touching
AA's institutional history are not, because that history is held in copyrighted
books I had not obtained at that point. Kurtz was subsequently read at source and moved
the Chapter 4 and 5 claims recorded below from reported to scholarly support. This paragraph
records the earlier acquisition state and is superseded by the 2 August update at the end
of the register.

**Updated again after Eddy 1887 and Crothers 1911**, both supplied by the author. Chapter 2
gains five claims at first hand: the Sons of Temperance founding call with its date, its fee
and its benefit schedule; Mitchell's position on the liquor traffic, quoted from Jewett; the
judgement that the movement was not irreligious; Crothers' five-million pledge figure with
its explicit *unknown number* concession; and the descent of the lodging houses into the
inebriate hospital system. **And one citation was found to be wrong rather than merely
unverified:** Crothers had been cited for the Sons of Temperance and does not mention them.

**What acquiring Marsh demonstrates about the remaining list.** Every source Maxwell cites
from the 1840s to the 1920s is out of copyright, and several are digitised. Marsh took under
an hour to find, download and read against the chapters, and it produced one upgraded
mechanism, one primary quotation, one resolved political claim and one new source conflict.
The same is likely true of Krout 1925, Eddy 1887, Blair 1888 and Fehlandt 1904, all of which
are currently cited through Maxwell. That is a cheap and unglamorous way to move Part One
from solid to well-sourced, and it does not need a library.
