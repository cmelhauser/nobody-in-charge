# Summary

Angrist's argument that peer effects estimated by regressing individual outcomes on group averages
are largely mechanical. Without covariates they are vacuous; with covariates the putative peer
effect is a comparison of two-stage least squares with ordinary least squares, which measurement
error and other forces unrelated to peers can drive apart (abstract; 1 to 12). Designs that move
peers independently of individual traits are the strong evidence, and they have found little (17
to 21). The paper cites it, beside Carrell, Sacerdote and West (2013), for the warning it takes
most seriously about interventions built on measured peer effects, and the working paper supports
that use: its section 5 (17) describes their reassignment of Air Force Academy peer groups, which
had no overall effect and marginally significant negative effects for the students it was meant to
help.

SHA-256 and a verification index. The published article (*Labour Economics* 30: 98-108) is
paginated differently, so a page reference here must be checked against it before it is cited. No
manuscript chapter cites the work.
