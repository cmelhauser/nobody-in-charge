# Summary

Bill W.'s April 1946 *A.A. Grapevine* article, the first publication of what became the Twelve
Traditions. Roughly 1,600 words: an argument that AA's experience should be recorded as tradition
rather than hardened into rules, a short history from the 1939 book to 24,000 members, and then the
twelve points themselves.

**What it settles for this project.** Chapter 5 quotes three sentences as its thesis, previously
taken from Kurtz at a remove. They are now read at source and are exact: "Nobody invented
Alcoholics Anonymous. It grew. Trial and error has produced a rich experience." It also explains
the two-title problem: the Grapevine headline and Bill's own internal heading for the list differ,
and Kurtz cites the latter.

**What it does not settle.** The scan carries no issue or page markers, so Kurtz's conflicting
2:10 and 2:11 citations remain unadjudicated.

**Rights.** The document is not archived here. It is A.A. Grapevine copyright, the located scan is
a third-party reproduction with unverified authorization, and the project's own rights review
directs that it be cited and quoted within limits rather than archived.
