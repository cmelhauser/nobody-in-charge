# Summary

A prospective analysis of Project MATCH data asking whether alcoholics who help other alcoholics
relapse less in the year after treatment. Kaplan-Meier survival estimates and proportional-hazards
regressions, controlling for the number of AA meetings attended.

**Finding.** Those helping other alcoholics were significantly less likely to relapse, and the
effect held independently of meeting attendance. Among helpers, 40 per cent avoided any drink in
the year after treatment; among non-helpers, 22 per cent. The difference appeared in both study
arms.

**What it may support in this project.** It is external empirical evidence for the *premise* the
model encodes: that helping is a distinct component of involvement rather than a by-product of
attendance. The model gives helping its own resource, recipient opportunity per high-practice
potential helper, and gives Step 12 its own ablation. Pagano is evidence that the premise is worth
encoding. It is not evidence that the model's implementation is right.

**What it may not support.** It is observational, not experimental, so it cannot establish that
helping *causes* the lower relapse rate; selection is the obvious alternative, and the paper says
so. The authors' own first limitation is that only 8 per cent of the sample were coded as helping,
on a crude measure. It concerns individual drinking outcomes after formal treatment, whereas this
project's model is about group-level dynamics and its practice scale has no validated individual
or clinical unit. Nothing here licenses advice to any individual, and the two must not be joined
into a claim that the model predicts recovery.
