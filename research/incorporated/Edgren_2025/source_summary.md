# Summary

A 91-day intensive longitudinal study of what happens to habit strength when someone sets out to
degrade a habit. 194 adults in Switzerland, recruited by social-media advertisement between
September 2022 and April 2023, each chose one of four health-risk behaviours (sedentary behaviour
46, unhealthy snacking 57, alcohol 52, smoking 39), observed their own cues for seven days, then
picked one cue and one strategy, substitution 58 per cent, inhibition 35 per cent or cue
discontinuation 6.7 per cent, and formed an implementation intention. Habit strength was reported
daily on the four-item Self-Report Behavioural Automaticity Index from day 7 to day 91, giving
11,805 observations.

Six models, constant, linear, quadratic, cubic, asymptotic and logistic, were fitted per person and
selected by BIC. The asymptotic and logistic models were most often the best fit, 54 per cent of
the sample between them. Where fitted values were valid and the trajectory crossed the scale
midpoint, time for decay to stabilise, defined as reaching 95 per cent of the lower asymptote
within the observed window and never by extrapolation, had a median of 9 to 10 days and a range of
1 to 65 days, in 42 people, 22 per cent of the sample. A multilevel model put 76 per cent of
variance between persons; initial habit strength differed by behaviour, higher for sedentary
behaviour at 3.06 and lower for alcohol at 1.90, but the decay trajectory itself did not.

What it supports here is a comparison and its limit. This is the first study to measure habit decay
in daily life at high resolution, so it is the closest measured object to the model's decay rate in
form. It is the furthest in meaning: it measures an unwanted habit weakening while the person works
to remove it, and the model's dial is a wanted practice fading because nothing renews it. The
authors themselves caution that stabilisation does not mean the habit is broken and that the
shortest estimates, one day, are not consistent with habit theory. They also report that habit
strength is distinct from cue-behaviour performance, and that for some participants automaticity
fell gradually while the behaviour was consistently not performed, which is the part of the paper
closest to what the model represents. Used in Chapter 12 and the paper's limitations as the fast
end of the bracket around the decay rate.
