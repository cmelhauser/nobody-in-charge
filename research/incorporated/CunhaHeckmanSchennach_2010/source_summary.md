# Summary

The empirical companion to Cunha and Heckman (2007). It writes each stage's production of
cognitive and noncognitive skill as a CES in five inputs, the child's two skills, investment and
the parent's two skills, with a substitution parameter φ for each stage and skill (6). It proves
nonparametric identification of such technologies from nonlinear factor models, drawing on
Schennach (2004a) and Hu and Schennach (2008) (3, 11 to 16); anchors latent skills in adult outcomes
(16 to 18); and allows for endogenous parental investment, first through time-invariant
heterogeneity and then through time-varying heterogeneity with family income as the excluded
variable (18 to 21, 29 to 30). Estimated on 2,207 firstborn white children in the Children of the
NLSY79, substitutability for cognitive skill falls sharply between early and late childhood while
that for noncognitive skill stays roughly constant (abstract; 25 to 31; Tables 1, 4 and 5).

**Read in full on 13 September 2026** in the working-paper version, with its tables; page
references are the working paper's, not *Econometrica*'s. It documents the paper's and Chapter 13's
statement that the formal apparatus was read at source. `research/SOURCES.md` records three
qualifications, on notation, on the word "multiplicative" and on "solve it", and one candidate
finding: the sign of the estimated substitution parameter changes between stages for cognitive
skill. All four were applied on 13 September 2026: Chapters 12 and 13 and the paper carry the
corrections, Chapter 13 and the paper the sign finding as an analogy, and Chapter 14 no longer
credits this paper with a depreciation structure its technology does not have.
