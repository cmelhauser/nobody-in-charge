# Summary

A prospective, fully lagged mediation analysis of why having a 12-step sponsor predicts better
drinking outcomes. 115 adults newly affiliated with 12-step programmes, recruited in New Mexico,
assessed at baseline and at 3, 6 and 9 months, with drinking measured by the Form 90 and the
abstinence-based social network by the Important People and Activities inventory.

Sponsorship at 3 months predicted proportion of days abstinent at 9 months, but the effect was not
carried by the social network: the association between sponsorship and the proportion of abstinent
and in-recovery contacts did not survive controlling for the baseline network, the network at 6
months predicted none of the 9-month substance-use measures, and the sponsorship effect remained
after controlling for the network. The authors note that the five earlier studies reporting
mediation used cross-sectional or partially lagged designs and found small mediated effects.
