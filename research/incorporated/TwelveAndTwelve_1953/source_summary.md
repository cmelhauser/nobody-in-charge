# Summary

AA's own book-length commentary on the Twelve Steps and the Twelve Traditions, written by Bill W
and published in 1953. It is the fellowship's authoritative interpretation of the twelve rules
this book is about, and until 10 August 2026 the project had argued about Traditions 2, 9 and 12
without reading what AA itself says they mean.

**Record only. No document is stored at any time**, on the same footing as pamphlet P-17. The
work is AAWS copyright, and this repository is public. AAWS publishes the book free, one chapter
per PDF, at aa.org; a reading copy was assembled locally from those files, read in full on
10 August 2026, and left outside the repository. `metadata.json` records the SHA-256 of that
assembly and of the printed pages verified.

## What it supports

**Tradition Two, printed pages 132-138.** The chapter states the book's own thesis in Bill W's
words: the group conscience "will be in the long run wiser than any single leader". It narrates
the founding case, in which the group overrules Bill's plan to become a paid lay therapist and he
defers. It describes the group's committee as "sharply limited" in authority, unable in any sense
to govern or direct, and its members as servants rather than senators.

**Tradition Nine, printed pages 174-178.** The long form states plainly that "Rotating leadership
is the best", which is a second and independent textual anchor for a claim the project had
previously sourced only to pamphlet P-17. The chapter warns the fellowship to avoid "those dangers
of great wealth, prestige, and entrenched power", which names the hazard the aggregation condition
formalizes.

**Tradition Twelve, printed pages 187-191.** The chapter describes two functions and treats them
as separate lessons learned at different times: confidentiality of a member's name and story, and
one hundred per cent anonymity at press, radio, film and television, adopted so that no
"self-appointed members" could "present themselves as messiahs representing A.A." The second is
the structural reading this book argues for, and the chapter's own framing shows it is not the
only job the Tradition does.

**The pairing test, computed rather than read.** No Tradition chapter cites the Step of its own
number, in any of the twelve. The twelve Step chapters contain no occurrence of the word
"Tradition" at all. The only indexed cross-reference in the book is Tradition Eight citing the
Twelfth Step, which is off-index. Chapter Sixteen previously had to record that it could not say
how strongly AA literature gestures at the index pairing; it does not gesture at it.

## What it costs the argument

**The elder statesmen passage is the strongest objection to this book's Tradition 2 claim, and it
comes from AA's own text.** Tradition Two's chapter says that deposed founders who mature become
"elder statesmen", that they are "the real and permanent leadership of A.A.", that they "become
the voice of the group conscience", and that when a group is "sorely perplexed" it "inevitably
turns to them for advice". Read against Golub and Jackson, that is a description of a persistent,
non-rotating influence concentration, and it is endorsed rather than warned against. It also
undercuts the rotation argument from a second direction: the positions the fellowship rotates are
the ones the same chapter says carry no authority.

The book must answer this rather than cite the source selectively.

## What it cannot support

Nothing about what groups in fact do. It is a retrospective interpretation written by one man
seventeen years after the fellowship began, and, as Kurtz observes in the talk catalogued at
`KurtzTalk_c1984`, it is "Bill's telling the story of his own thinking". Its stories are told to
illustrate principles, not sampled from practice. Treat it as evidence of what the Traditions are
understood to mean, never as evidence of adherence or of behaviour.

## Note on the verification index

`subjects_present` is computed with the project's OCR-tolerant matcher, which over-includes short
tokens. Long registered phrases in the index are reliable; five-letter ones are not, and this is a
property of the shared matcher rather than of this source.
