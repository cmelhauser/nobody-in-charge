# Summary

A Franciscan priest's book-length devotional reading of the Twelve Steps as Christian
spirituality. Read in full on 10 August 2026 as an example of how the Steps are received by a
religiously-informed reader outside the fellowship.

**Record only. No document is stored at any time.**

## The provenance problem, stated plainly

The file consulted carries an `OceanofPDF.com` imprint at every chapter break. That is an
unauthorized distribution site, and *Breathing Under Water* is a current in-print commercial
title, so the posting was plainly not authorized by the rights holder. This is a stronger
objection than the two unverified-authorization cases already in the corpus, DeGroot (1974) and
the 1946 *Grapevine*, where the project could say only that authorization was unconfirmed.

The project's response, recorded so a reader can judge it:

- the file is not retained, not committed, and not mirrored;
- it is not quoted at length anywhere in the manuscript;
- the bibliographic record in `citation.md` was confirmed against publisher and library listings
  independently of the file, so nothing cited depends on the unauthorized copy being accurate;
- every claim drawn from it is an unquantified statement about how the book reads its subject,
  which is the weakest use the four-layer evidence rule permits;
- the two claims the manuscript actually makes are both **absence** claims, verifiable by anyone
  holding a lawful copy without needing this one.

Any quotation must be checked against a lawfully obtained edition before release.

## What it supports

**A book-length treatment of the Steps that never mentions the Traditions.** The word does not
appear in the body of the book. Rohr cites *Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions* exactly once, in
an endnote to the twelfth Step. For Chapter Sixteen's argument this is a second, independent
demonstration that the Steps and the Traditions are read as different objects by people who take
the Steps extremely seriously.

**An alternative reading of anonymity, held by a serious reader.** Rohr's chapter on Step Nine
reads anonymity as protection of the vulnerable and a discipline against gossip, under the heading
"Anonymity and Total Disclosure", and he asks whether that is "part of why the word 'anonymous' is
in the very A.A. title". Chapter Eight's notes on sources already conceded that someone could
reasonably argue anonymity is primarily protective and only incidentally structural. Rohr is that
someone, and the objection is no longer hypothetical.

Usefully, the two readings do not compete: Tradition Twelve's own chapter in the Twelve and Twelve
names both functions and treats them as separate lessons.

## What it cannot support

Nothing whatever about what AA groups do. There is no fieldwork in it, no data, and no discussion
of governance. It is one author's theology. It may be cited for how the Steps are read and for
what it does not contain, and for nothing else.
