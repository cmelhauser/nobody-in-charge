# Source summary: SMF-132, estimated worldwide A.A. membership

**Read in full on 17 August 2026** from the official aa.org posting. A one-page table of
reported active membership and group counts, 1935 to 2020, with a note on method.

## What it is for

`HANDOFF.md` listed this as needed for the out-of-sample comparison Chapter 21 proposes. It is
the only published series giving both a membership count and a group count for the same years,
which is what a mean group size requires.

## The numbers that matter here

Members per group, formed as reported members divided by reported groups:

| Year | Members | Groups | Members per group |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1950 | 96,475 | 3,527 | 27.4 |
| 1970 | 311,450 | 16,459 | 18.9 |
| 1990 | 2,047,469 | 93,914 | 21.8 |
| 2000 | 2,160,013 | 100,766 | 21.4 |
| 2010 | 2,057,672 | 107,976 | 19.1 |
| 2020 | 2,138,201 | 129,790 | 16.5 |

Across 2001 to 2020 the ratio has a mean of 18.4 and a range of 16.5 to 22.1, declining steadily
as groups are added faster than members.

## What this can and cannot be compared to

The model's endpoint membership at full adherence is 17.80, with a 95 per cent interval of
[16.92, 18.68] on 400 seeds at thirty years. That sits inside the observed range and near its
mean.

**This is a consistency check and not a validation, for a reason the source itself supplies.**
A.A. keeps no membership lists; the figures are reports from groups registered with general
service offices. The ratio of two estimated aggregates is not a sample of group sizes, carries no
interval, and says nothing about a distribution that is certainly skewed. The model produces a
distribution and the source produces a point. They agree at the point, which is worth one
sentence and not a chapter.

The model was not fitted to this. Room capacity of 60 and the arrival and dropout rates were set
long before this source was obtained, which is what makes the comparison out-of-sample at all.
