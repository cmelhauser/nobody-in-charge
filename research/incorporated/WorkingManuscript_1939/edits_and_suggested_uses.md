# The 1939 working manuscript: what the edits show, and where they could go

*Working note for the Human Author, 12 September 2026. Source: `WorkingManuscript_1939`.*

Every facsimile page of Hazelden's *The Book That Started It All* (2010) was read from the 241
photographs and every change that mattered was checked against the 1939
text already in the corpus (`BigBook_1939`). Manuscript pages are cited by the number Hazelden prints
beside each facsimile ("MS p. 30"). Hazelden's own essays are cited as Hazelden's claims, and anything
they quote from AA's copyrighted histories is at a remove. Quotations are kept to the few words needed
to identify an edit: this is a map to the pages, not a transcription of them. Early members are named
by initial, as elsewhere in the project.

---

## In brief

1. **The edits change who is speaking far more than how firmly.** Across the programme chapters,
   instruction to "you" becomes a report of what "we" did. The modal usually survives: typed "You must
   take the lead" became "we found we had to" in pencil and "We must take the lead" in print (MS
   p. 41). Nowhere does "you must" become "we ought", which is how Chapter 4 describes the change.
2. **The qualifier on God is older than the comment round in one place and younger in two.** "As we
   understood Him" is typed in Step 3 of the circulated draft. In Step 11 it is written in by hand
   over a struck "a Power above us" (MS pp. 30-31). And the "Why don't you choose your own conception
   of God?" episode in Bill's Story is a handwritten insert on two loose leaves, keyed into MS p. 10,
   that the circulated draft did not contain.
3. **"Group" comes out wherever the text speaks as a body.** "As a group" is struck twice, "Members of
   our group" once, a hope that "our group" would reform institutions goes with its paragraph, and
   readers flag Oxford Group vocabulary with "GRP", "Group" and "Too Groupy". At galley stage a
   "non-denominational group" became "non-denominational people" and a "rule" became a "principle".
   The one expansion runs the same way: "the Fellowship" became "Fellowships", plural, in each city
   and hamlet.
4. **The Foreword already held the substance of four Traditions,** typed before any comment came in:
   anonymity at the public level, not an organization in the conventional sense, no fees or dues, and
   one requirement for membership. Beside it the draft proposed a central trust whose outside trustees
   would "always constitute a majority" and whose approval every business engagement would need.
5. **The collective process narrowed at the end.** As many as four hundred copies went out,
   deliberately to outsiders as well as members, but the comments were collated onto one copy, chiefly
   in one hand by Hazelden's account, under an author who called himself the umpire, and the last
   changes were made by four people correcting galleys. One outside reader, the psychiatrist Dr.
   Howard, is named legibly in the margins on seven pages, and probably on an eighth; some of his
   changes went in and more did not.
6. **Ten of the printed book's thirty stories were not in the circulated draft.** A pencilled list
   inside the back cover names them in print order. The one story struck through with a withdrawal
   note was printed anyway, moved from the middle of the sequence to the end.
7. **Members' own figures put early-1939 meetings between thirty and eighty people.** A consistency
   check on the model's room of sixty and nothing more.

## The document

**What it is.** In mid-February 1939 the draft was run off by multilith, a small offset process, and
as many as four hundred copies, stamped as loan copies, went to members, doctors, clergy and others.
Wilson later said the point was to find anything that "goes against the grain". The comments were
written onto one copy, this one, over about six weeks before the final edit.

**Hands.** Lead, green and red pencil, a little ink. "HGP", Hank P.'s initials by Hazelden's reading,
stands at the foot of nearly every text page; the Sotheby's catalogue that Hazelden quotes gives most
annotations to him and some to Wilson. Readers are named in the margins: Dr. Howard on MS pp. 10, 11,
18, 19, 23, 46 and 86, and probably on p. 56, where the name is too faint to be sure at the
resolution checked; a "Dr. Witherspoon" on p. 17 as far as the pencil can be read, and a reader
whose name begins "De B" on the Foundation page, who found it anticlimactic. A red note on p. 21 cites
"our away gang", which Hazelden reads as the Akron members. Nothing here attributes a hand beyond what
the page signs.

**Printer's marks.** MS p. 1 is marked "1 Printer" and p. 58 "Copy for type setter"; every story page
from p. 81 carries "Printer" and its number, in black to p. 126 and in green from p. 127. Hazelden's
first essay argues from the near illegibility of p. 43 that this was not the typesetter's copy. The
marks suggest that at least the story section was prepared for the typesetter; they leave the heavily
edited text pages open.

**Three states, not two.** For some sentences the draft, the pencil and print all differ. Hazelden's
example is MS p. 38, where "You will know you are on the Broad Highway" became "We knew we were" and
then, in print, "We feel we are". The galley stage made changes this copy does not record, and some of
them put back force the pencil had taken out.

**The baseline, and two missing pages.** `BigBook_1939` is a 1999 reprint. It carries one bracketed
editorial note quoting the multilith, and it omits the 1939 appendix on The Alcoholic Foundation, so
the manuscript's Foundation page cannot be compared with print from the corpus. Book pages 22 and 23
were not photographed; they are probably the multilith title page and Lois W.'s presentation leaf,
both described in Hazelden's essays. The OCR text held beside the PDF is a search aid only.

## What the edits change

### 1. Instruction becomes testimony

The heaviest and most consistent change. Whole paragraphs move from "you" to "we" and from present to
past (the Foreword, MS p. 16, and pp. 25 to 44 almost throughout), and three families of phrase go
with them.

- **Selling.** "Designed to sell you three pertinent ideas" becomes "make clear" (p. 31); getting
  "thoroughly sold on the idea" of being a candidate for the fellowship is struck (p. 18); "The kick
  you will get is tremendous" is struck (p. 45); "Try it" is struck after "It works" (p. 44).
- **The ultimatum.** A reader not yet convinced was told to re-read the book "or else throw it
  away"; the sentence is circled out (p. 31).
- **Kneeling.** "Get down upon your knees" before the Third Step prayer is struck (p. 32), and "on
  our knees" leaves Step 7 (p. 30). Wilson's later gloss, which Hazelden quotes, was that the draft
  had readers on their knees and needed to stand them up.

The readers said why. "Should be studied from the mold angle" heads How It Works (p. 30); a margin on
p. 32 notes that Bill is known as someone who does not want to pour people into moulds; p. 43 has
"POURING THE MOLD"; and the endpaper (p. 156) asks why, if organized religion's fault is pouring
people into moulds, the book should give specific instructions at all. The metaphor is the book's own.
*The Unbeliever*, typed in the same draft, complains that the preachers tried "to pour everyone into
moulds" (p. 89, and again p. 91). The reviewers turned the text's complaint against religion back on
the text.

**What happened to the modal.** This is where Chapter 4 needs changing.

- p. 41: typed "You must take the lead" became "we found we had to"; print has "We must take the lead".
- p. 41: typed "you ought to say" lost its "ought to" in pencil; print has "We feel we ought to say".
- p. 42: typed "you ought to leave them alone" became "we ought"; print has "we think we ought not to
  urge them".
- p. 30: "You must find Him now!" became "May you find Him now!", the one clear softening of force.

The shift is from command to report, and the galleys put some of the force back in the first person.
The manuscript contains no "you must" that became "we ought".

**One edit runs the other way, and the manuscript explains it.** In We Agnostics, "WHO AM I TO SAY
THERE IS NO GOD?" became "are you" (p. 29). The episode being retold is Fitz M.'s, and his own typed
story has the voice ask "Who are you to say there is no God?" (p. 106). The change matched the chapter
to its source.

**Where the second person stayed.** Working with Others keeps its "you" (pp. 45 to 51), as Hazelden
notes; its reader is already recovered and is being told how to approach somebody else.

### 2. God, and whose conception

- **Step 3** is typed "care and direction of God as we understood Him". "And direction" is struck;
  the qualifier stands, underlined, consistent with the italics it is printed in (p. 30).
- **Step 11** is typed "improve our contact with God". "Conscious" is added, and "as we understand
  Him" is written in by hand in place of a struck "a Power above us" (p. 31).
- **Steps 1, 6, 7, 8 and 12.** "We" is added before "Admitted"; "willing" becomes "ready"; "on our
  knees" and "holding nothing back" are struck from Step 7, and "complete" from Step 8's amends; Step
  12 exchanges "this course of action" for "these steps" and drops "others, especially" before
  "alcoholics". A pencilled "to our understanding of God" in Step 5, and a query whether "anxious"
  would show more humility in Step 6, were not taken. Step 12's "experience" became "awakening" only
  in the second printing of 1941, per Hazelden.
- **Bill's Story.** The episode in which Ebby T. suggests Wilson choose his own conception of God,
  with the objection to "a Czar of the Heavens" and the sentence about being willing to believe in a
  power greater than himself, is written out in pencil on two leaves bound at the front and keyed by
  "Inst #1" into MS p. 10. There the typed text runs straight from "His roots grasped a new soil" to
  "Thus was I convinced". Print has the insert. What was already typed matters as much: p. 10 has "as
  I then understood Him", and We Agnostics has "we mean your own conception of God", untouched
  (p. 25). The idea was in the draft; its grounding in Wilson's own story was added. A reader on p. 25
  suggested moving those We Agnostics paragraphs back into Bill's Story. They stayed where they were,
  and whether the suggestion prompted the insert cannot be shown.
- **Dr. Howard.** "Word God is a limiting word" heads p. 11, where "God has to work twenty-four hours
  a day" became "Faith has to work", with a margin asking who we are to say what God has to do. That
  change went in. Most of his other suggestions to put "faith" for "God" did not (pp. 9, 10), and
  "Heavenly Father" stayed in Dr Bob's story against "Dr Howard says change" (p. 86).
- **Jim B.'s claim.** Hazelden quotes Jim B.'s later account that his one contribution was qualifying
  God with "as we understand Him". The typed Step 3 shows the qualifier in the text before the comment
  round, so whatever he contributed came during drafting, not revision.
- **Fitz M.'s story.** The member Wilson says wanted the more religious book wrote the story with the
  most open formula in the draft: a power called "God, Allah, Confucius, Prime Cause, Divine Mind, or
  any other name" (p. 105).

### 3. The group comes out of the text

- "As a group" is struck twice on p. 17, once before "we have no desire to convince anyone" and once
  from "no concern of ours ... what religious bodies" members belong to.
- "Members of our group have spent thousands of dollars" loses "Members of our group" (p. 37).
- A paragraph on institutions ending in the hope that "our group" would help change them is struck
  whole (p. 56).
- Readers flag Oxford Group vocabulary: "shared" circled with "GRP" (p. 12), a crossed-out "Group"
  over Step 3 (p. 30), "Buchman" beside Step 11 (p. 31), "Oxford GROUP" and "This is absolutely Too
  Groupy" on the Eleventh Step page (p. 43), and two "Group" flags on p. 77.
- At galley stage two more went: "As a non-denominational group, we cannot make up people's minds",
  typed and unmarked on p. 64, is printed as "non-denominational people"; and "Another rule we observe
  carefully", typed on p. 61, is printed as "Another principle".
- The one expansion: the hope that "the Fellowship" would spring up became "Fellowships", plural, "in
  each city and hamlet" (p. 75).

Part of this is shedding the Oxford Group, and the readers say so. The effect is wider: the text stops
speaking as a body that holds positions. The draft already said as much in its own typed voice, and
those sentences were kept. It does not want to be "the arbiter of anyone's sex conduct" (p. 35); it
wishes "to lay down no rule of any sort" (p. 41); it has "no monopoly on God" (p. 48); on serving
liquor at home, "We never argue this question" and each family should decide for itself (p. 51); its
suggestions carry "nothing obligatory" (p. 64); the book is by no means the last word (p. 69) and is
"meant to be suggestive only" (p. 79). The stories say the same of the gatherings: "nothing
organized", and "fatal to give it any kind of a tag" (p. 90); a meeting "without formality" (p. 121);
"none of the crowd were missionaries" (p. 113).

### 4. Deference to doctors and clergy

Mostly Dr. Howard's, and after the pronouns the largest class of edit, as Hazelden's own listing also
finds. Medical claims are hedged: "is certain to" die becomes "may" (p. 15), and "fatally ill" becomes
"very ill", beside the note "Doctors will shoot" (p. 45). Criticism of psychiatrists goes: "often
fruitlessly, we are afraid" (p. 12) and "bitter" (p. 16). A member is armed no longer with "certain
medical information" but with "the facts about himself", after a margin calling doctors "a jealous lot"
(p. 12). The paragraph surveying detoxification methods is struck whole after being edited once, so
there were two passes (p. 69). "Most doctors" becomes "Many" (p. 23). And "new" is taken out of
"something new in the way of a solution" because "Ministers will shoot", millions having been cured
through evangelism (p. 46). This is the manuscript's clearest precursor of having no opinion on outside
issues, and it was argued out margin by margin.

### 5. Helping others

- **The helping claim was defended in editing.** The doctor's letter said the solution was worked out
  on the spiritual plane. A reader added "as well as an altruistic" and wrote above it that this frees
  Wilson from the criticism that he says you cannot get well unless you work with others (p. 2). Print
  has it.
- **"Helping others is the foundation stone of your recovery"** is pencilled over the typed opening
  of that sentence (p. 48), and printed.
- **The burden was stated down.** Typed "This sort of thing goes on constantly" became "Occasionally
  you will have to meet such conditions" (p. 48). The draft already warned that those who shoulder "the
  entire burden and trouble of others" are soon overcome (p. 64).
- **The motive, typed.** The first members knew they must help others to stay sober, but "that motive
  became secondary", transcended by the happiness of giving (p. 77).
- **In the stories.** Dr Bob's fourth reason, insurance against a slip (p. 86); "he owed me, I didn't
  owe him" (p. 90); visiting as "part of the 'treatment'" (p. 95); and a doctor who assigned the calls,
  asking only that the new man tell his story (p. 144).

### 6. Organization and money

- **The Foreword**, typed: the request that members stay anonymous in public, with the press asked to
  observe it; not an organization in the conventional sense; no fees or dues; the only requirement an
  honest desire to stop drinking; allied with no faith and opposing no one. A reader added that any
  notion of a pat on the back should be separated out, since "the field needs workers". An offer to
  send the multilith itself for $3.50 is struck.
- **A handwritten list of tasks** bound at the front (book p. 26) runs from rewriting the business
  chapter to "How about Alcoholic Foundation set up?", and includes chocolate for the craving. That item
  became the published paragraph citing a doctor who read the book "in manuscript form", the one place
  the 1939 book mentions its own comment round. The "Sugar" notes on MS pp. 45, 47, 49 and 65, and "Go
  back on sugar pages" on p. 59, are its trail through the draft.
- **The Alcoholic Foundation page**, unnumbered, after MS p. 80: a trust to receive inquiries and
  royalties; three trustees from the fellowship and four from outside, the four and their successors
  to "always constitute a majority"; "our only agency of its kind"; the trustees' approval for every
  business engagement touching the work; and a plan to answer inquiries by referring them to "an
  active nucleus" in each locality until fellowships spring up there. The proposed centre was small,
  permanent, controlled from outside, and routed growth through itself. Whether the printed appendix
  kept all of this cannot be checked from the corpus.
- **Money, at a remove**, from Hazelden's history and Wilson's 1954 talk: Works Publishing's $25
  shares; the Harper advance the Foundation board wanted taken and Wilson and Hank P. turned down; and
  an Akron meeting of about eighteen that feared the project would "create a professional class" and
  let Wilson go ahead by a majority of two or three.

### 7. The stories

- The draft has nineteen stories and a twentieth struck through. The pencilled list inside the back
  cover adds ten more, with page ranges and the writers' names (not reproduced here), in the order print
  gives them: A Close Shave; "Intellectual Agnostic", printed as Educated Agnostic; Another Prodigal
  Story; The Car Smasher; Hindsight; On His Way; An Alcoholic's Wife; An Artist's Concept; The Rolling
  Stone; and, as far as the pencil can be read, Lone Endeavor.
- **Ace Full, Seven, Eleven** has a green X through all four of its pages and a note, initialled
  W.G.W. by Hazelden's reading, that Del T. thought the book a racket and withdrew it. The manuscript's
  page numbering skips it. The 1939 book prints it anyway, as the last story, after Lone Endeavor,
  rather than after Riding the Rods where the draft had it. The simplest reading is withdrawal during
  revision and restoration at the very end. The pages cannot say when or why, and the note may postdate
  either.
- The stories were barely edited. Suggested cuts to The Doctor's Nightmare, with running word counts of
  122 and 219, were not taken (pp. 81-82); a pseudo-medical claim about a foundation's research was
  struck from The Unbeliever (p. 88); the opening of Fired Again is boxed with a query (p. 145).
- For Appendix A13. The typed stories' arrivals are almost all member-carried: through a doctor who was
  himself a member, a family broker, or a fellow patient, in one case a member who had himself relapsed
  and was going back to try again (p. 140). The one arrival by book alone, Lone Endeavor, is among the
  late additions, and its subject was reached with a copy of this multilith. And the line in To
  Employers that "a great deal can be accomplished by the use of the book alone", until personal
  contact is possible, is a pencilled addition that print kept (p. 70).

## What the manuscript cannot tell you

- **Who.** Four or five hands, and only initials and a few names on the page.
- **When.** Annotations are undated. A change's presence shows that someone proposed it, not when or on
  whose authority, and some notes, Ace Full's perhaps, may be later.
- **Why**, beyond the reasons the margins give.
- **Whether this was the printer's copy.** The marks point both ways.
- **What the printed appendix said.** The corpus's 1939 text lacks it.

Nothing here changes a model value, a cache or a release check.

## Suggested uses in the manuscript

In rough order of value. The first, a correction, was made on 13 September 2026: Chapter 4 now says
what the pages show. The recount in item 9 was made the same day: Appendix A13 now counts thirty
stories and records how the working manuscript accounts for them.

The other seven were drafted on 15 September 2026 at the Human Author's request, each as its own
commit so that any one can be dropped on its own, and every quotation and page reference was checked
against the page images:

- item 2, Chapter 5;
- item 3, Chapters 9 and 7, with Chapter 7 given one sentence of cross-reference;
- item 4, Chapter 18;
- item 5, Chapter 15;
- item 6, Chapter 19;
- item 7, Chapter 22;
- item 8, Chapters 24 and 21.

Three parts were left out:

- **From item 3:** the title vote and the four people at the galleys. Both reach the book only
  through Hazelden's reading of AA's histories, and the addition stands without them.
- **From item 4:** household autonomy on serving liquor, too weak a match for group autonomy.
- **From item 6:** the Bill's Story insert, since Step Eleven's handwritten qualifier makes the same
  point.

Dr. Howard is counted as named on seven pages, not eight. On MS p. 56 the name is too faint at the
resolution checked.

1. **Chapter 4, a correction.** Replace "the softening of 'you must' to 'we ought'" with what the pages
   show: instruction to "you" rewritten as a report of what "we" did, the modal usually kept, and some
   force restored at galley stage ("We must take the lead"). The rest of that paragraph can now be
   first-hand for the manuscript layer: some four hundred copies to members and outsiders, collation on
   one copy, a psychiatrist named in the margins, the kneeling prayer and the ultimatum struck.
2. **Chapter 5, Twelve Points.** The Foreword typed in February 1939 already holds the substance of
   what became four Traditions, six years before the mail problem the chapter opens with. That sharpens
   the chapter's account: 1945 and 1946 generalized and codified principles the founders had written
   down at the start. The same 1939 text proposed the opposite arrangement for the centre, a permanent
   board with an outside majority whose approval every business engagement needed.
3. **Chapters 7 and 9, How a Room Decides and Confident and Wrong.** The founding text was not decided
   by a room. Wide solicitation fed a single collator, an author who called himself the umpire, and four
   people at the galleys. One outside reader is named on seven pages, and probably an eighth. Akron's group is twice
   described in its own stories as centred on its doctor, once in so many words as its "human center"
   (pp. 128, 132). And, at a remove, a title vote in the two groups was set aside, a member recalling
   that Doc and Bill let everyone fight and then announced the answer. These are Chapter 9's prominent
   agents in the fellowship's first months, described by members without complaint, and they make a
   historical foil for the rule the Traditions later wrote. It is an analogy and not a test: a one-pass
   editorial collation is not repeated averaging.
4. **Chapter 18, Two Kinds of Rule.** The five structural Traditions have 1939 precursors being
   negotiated on these pages: household autonomy on serving liquor (p. 51), the struck ambition to
   reform institutions (p. 56), the removal of claims doctors and ministers would contest (pp. 12 to
   46), "rule" becoming "principle" at galley stage (p. 61), and set against all of it the Foundation
   page's central approval. It would let the chapter show that having no hierarchy was chosen against a
   live alternative written into the founding draft.
5. **Chapter 15, Helping Is Not the Reward.** The "altruistic plane" edit and its stated reason (p. 2),
   "Helping others is the foundation stone" added in pencil (p. 48), the burden stated down (p. 48), the
   typed claim that the motive became secondary (p. 77), and the doctor who assigned calls (p. 144). The
   editors defended the necessity of helping as doctrine while softening its cost, which is close to the
   chapter's distinction between helping as mechanism and helping as reward.
6. **Chapter 19, You Cannot Close the Door.** The single requirement typed in the Foreword; the open
   conception strengthened in revision; relapsed members taken back "as though nothing had happened"
   (p. 122) and by friends who never lectured (p. 128); and Dr Bob's one precondition, whether the man
   wanted to stop (p. 131).
7. **Chapter 22, What You Cannot Engineer.** The founders' growth plan was to find an active nucleus in
   each locality and route inquiries to it (the Foundation page); the draft reports small clusters of two
   to five around two centres (p. 79); and Silkworth wrote in 1939, as Hazelden quotes him, that a
   community with three or four active members would grow. The chapter's question about founding
   composition had a 1939 answer in the founders' own heads.
8. **Chapters 24 and 21.** Chapter 24 asks whether dominance scales with room size and says the
   measurement would be intrusive. The 1939 record describes one room of sixty to eighty centred on one
   person; it cannot say how that scaled, but it shows the configuration in the fellowship's first rooms.
   For Chapter 21, the table above is a second consistency check beside SMF-132, eight decades earlier.
9. **Appendix A13.** Three additions and a recount. Ten of the thirty 1939 stories were gathered after
   the draft circulated, so the census mixes the draft's nineteen with ten late additions and one
   restored story. The one arrival by book alone, Lone Endeavor, is a late addition whose subject received
   this multilith. The book-alone line in To Employers was added in pencil, so the fellowship expected the
   book to work alone as a stopgap even while, in Lone Endeavor's frame, it doubted that. And A13 says
   twenty-nine stories where the 1939 contents page in `BigBook_1939` lists thirty titles from The
   Doctor's Nightmare to Ace Full.

## Loose ends

- Book pages 22 and 23 are still to be photographed. When they arrive they go through the same
  cleanup and into the reading copy, which will then have two more pages than photographs listed here.
- Hazelden's essays quote AA's own histories, chiefly *Alcoholics Anonymous Comes of Age*, *Pass It On*
  and *Dr. Bob and the Good Oldtimers*. Anything taken from those quotations belongs under "Cited at a
  remove". The *Comes of Age* passage that `HANDOFF.md` section 10 still wants, pages 97 to 98, is not
  among those Hazelden quotes.
