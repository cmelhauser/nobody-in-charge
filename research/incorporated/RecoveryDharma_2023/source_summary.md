# Summary

The program literature of Recovery Dharma, a peer-led Buddhist recovery fellowship. The second
edition is a collective volunteer product with no named author, published by Recovery Dharma
Global in 2023 under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. It is organized around the three jewels: Buddha, Dharma and
Sangha, with the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path as the program's spine, fourteen personal
recovery stories, and an appendix holding meditations, inquiry questions, a glossary, a meeting
format and a dedication of merit.

It was read on 16 August 2026 to answer one question: whether the group model says anything about a
recovery fellowship organized on Buddhist rather than Twelve Step lines, and in particular whether
the model supports the claim that the Dharma is a simplification of the Twelve Steps. The answer to
the second question is no, and the reasons are set out in appendix section A12.

**Held on the ordinary footing**, git-ignored with a SHA-256 and a verification index. Worth one
note: the licence expressly permits sharing and adaptation with attribution, so this is the single
source in the corpus that could lawfully be committed. It is git-ignored anyway, because the rule is
uniform. Provenance is a two-step record. The copy first read was an ephemeral session attachment
removed from disk before it could be stored; the user's own copy was then located and confirmed the
same document by page count, byte size and verbatim spot-checks, and that is the copy hashed here.

**Read in part.** Section I, the meeting format, the glossary and the dedication of merit were read
in full. The fourteen personal stories and the selected meditations and inquiry questions were not.
Every claim below comes from the part that was read.

## What it supports

**Its own structure, which is the whole of the use made of it.** The program's commitments are
distributed across seven enumerated lists rather than one: the three jewels, the Four Noble Truths
(printed 8), the eight factors of the Eightfold Path (16), the Five Precepts (29), the four heart
practices (21-24), the four foundations of mindfulness (35-36), and the seven commitments of "The
Practice" (xv-xvi, reprinted in the meeting script at 148).

**That the governance functions live in a meeting script rather than a charter.** The Recovery
Dharma Meeting Format (147-151) carries, as procedure, several of the functions AA assigns to
numbered Traditions: confidentiality in the closing, self-support through the dāna basket in the
announcements, a disclaimer of authority by the facilitator in the opening, and a no-crosstalk rule
in the group sharing. The script's own header states that it is "meant to serve as a suggested
template" and that individual meetings may edit it.

**That self-identification is optional by design.** The meeting format's introductions say there is
no need to identify yourself by anything other than your name (150), and the Sangha chapter states
that there is never any requirement to identify yourself in any way (43). This is a deliberate
departure from the ritual first-person identification that AA's Step One carries.

**That the helper threshold is low and uncertified.** Mentors are not a formal position and nobody
is certified or authorized to be one (49); anyone with any period of renunciation and practice can
be of service (xvi, 148). Where mentors are unavailable, wise friends may pair as partners.

## What it costs the argument

Nothing directly, because nothing in the manuscript depends on it. Its effect is to sharpen a
limitation the appendix already records. The model's eight resources were coded against the Twelve
Steps and Twelve Traditions, and this source shows that a functioning recovery fellowship can
distribute the same functions across a script, a commitments list, a practice taxonomy and a
community chapter. That is evidence that the model's architecture, which needs one Step matrix and
one Tradition matrix, encodes AA's document structure and not recovery-group structure in general.

## What it cannot support

Nothing about what Recovery Dharma groups do, no outcome, and no effectiveness comparison with AA.
It is a statement of intent by a fellowship about itself.

It cannot support the derivation claim in either direction. The Eightfold Path predates the Twelve
Steps by roughly two and a half thousand years, and the book presents its program as an application
of early Buddhist teaching rather than as a rewriting of anything. The book mentions Twelve Step
programs twice in what was read, once to say the inquiries can be used the way inventories are used
in Twelve Step programs and that some are not (50), and once to say the program is fully compatible
with other spiritual paths and programs of recovery (xi).

There is no Traditions-equivalent charter. That absence claim rests on the full table of contents,
the whole of Section I, and the whole of the Section III appendix except the meditations and
inquiry questions. A governance charter would be a listed item in the contents and is not there.

## Note on the verification index

`subjects_present` reports `chase`, `utica` and `washingtonian`. All three are artifacts of the
shared OCR-tolerant matcher over-including short tokens, the same limitation the
`TwelveAndTwelve_1953` note records, and none is a real subject of this book. The artifact is inert
because no chapter cites this source, so no pair is adjudicated against it. The directory token
`RecoveryDharma` was chosen to be distinctive and inert: it is at least three characters, and the
bare word does not occur in the manuscript, so no pair is manufactured. The obvious alternative
token, "Dharma", was rejected because it appears throughout this source's own vocabulary and would
pair indiscriminately, which is the failure mode the Grosh and Maxwell notes in `check_book.py`
warn against.
