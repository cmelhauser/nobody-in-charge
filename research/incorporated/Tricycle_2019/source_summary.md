# Source summary: Tricycle on the 2019 Refuge Recovery split

**Read in full on 17 August 2026** at the publisher's site. `HANDOFF.md` listed the absence of an
independent account of this split as an open item, because everything Appendix A12.4 said about
it came from the successor fellowship's own literature, written by people who left.

## What it independently confirms

Two nonprofit organizations came out of the 2019 split. One continued the predecessor's name and
retained associated teacher-led meditation retreats and professional treatment options. The other
was formed as an alternative without those. Individual sanghas chose which to affiliate with.

That establishes the structural fact the appendix relies on, from outside the successor's own
account of itself.

## What it corrects

**The appendix drew the line in the wrong place.** It is not that the successor is peer-led and
the predecessor was not. Both describe their meetings as peer-led and democratically run, and the
predecessor's own book did so before the split. What separates them is the layer above the
meeting: teacher-led retreats and a professional treatment option on one side, nothing above the
sangha on the other.

That fits what this book models better than the original claim did. The model has no
representation of a meeting's internal democracy, which both fellowships share. It has a
governance layer that can be concentrated or not, and the split is precisely about whether such a
layer exists above the group.

## What is deliberately not taken from it

The article names the predecessor's founder and refers to the allegations surrounding the split.
This project names no one and adjudicates nothing. The structural claim does not need the name,
and `tools/check_book.py` now fails the build if it appears.
