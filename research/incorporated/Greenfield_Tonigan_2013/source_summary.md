# Summary

A measurement study of step-work. 130 new AA affiliates assessed at intake and at 3, 6 and 9
months, comparing a face-valid instrument, the Alcoholics Anonymous Inventory, with an indirect
one, the General Alcoholics Anonymous Tools of Recovery, and asking whether step-work predicts
later substance use.

**Findings.** Significantly more participants endorsed step-work on the GAATOR than on the AAI for
nine of the twelve steps. An exploratory factor analysis gave a two-factor structure, Behavioral
Step-Work and Spiritual Step-Work. Behavioral step-work did not change over time and was predicted
by having a sponsor. Spiritual step-work declined over time, and increases in it were predicted by
attending meetings or treatment. Behavioral step-work did not prospectively predict substance use;
spiritual step-work predicted percent days abstinent.

**What it may support in this project.** It is the strongest external evidence for a caveat the
book already makes on its own authority: that the model's practice scale is cardinal only inside
the model and corresponds to no validated instrument. Two instruments disagree about nine of the
twelve steps, so even the measurement is unsettled.

It also raises a real problem for the model rather than supporting it, which is the more useful
contribution. The model gives each step a single practice level. This study finds step-work is at
least two-dimensional, with the two dimensions having different predictors, different time paths,
and different relationships to outcome. A model with one number per step cannot represent a
member whose behavioral step-work holds steady while their spiritual step-work falls away, and
that is the pattern the study reports. The declining component is also the one that predicted
abstinence.

**What it may not support.** N is 130, the horizon is nine months, and the design is
observational. It concerns individual step-work and individual substance use, not group dynamics.
Nothing in it validates or refutes any group-level result in this project, and nothing in it
licenses advice to an individual.
