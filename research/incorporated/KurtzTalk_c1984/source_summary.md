# Summary

A recorded talk by Ernest Kurtz, the historian of Alcoholics Anonymous, drawing on Bill Wilson's
letters. Transcript restored by historyofrecovery.com. Read in full on 10 August 2026.

**Held since 13 September 2026; record only before that.** The talk is sold as an audiobook, and
the rights position of the transcript restored online was never established by the project, which
was the reason it was not held. The copy now held is a different thing: the Human Author's own
transcription, git-ignored at their direction and never committed. `metadata.json` records the
change under `formerly_record_only`.

**The year is inferred and must always be given as "about 1984".** See `citation.md` for the
internal evidence. This is a different item from Kurtz (1991), catalogued at `Kurtz_1991`.

## What it supports

**A provenance warning about the Twelve and Twelve.** Kurtz describes that book, and especially
its treatment of Steps Five, Six and Seven, as "Bill's telling the story of his own thinking"
during his depression. That is the right frame for the source catalogued at
`TwelveAndTwelve_1953`: authoritative as interpretation, not a record of group practice.

**A counterweight to this book's structural thesis, from the best-placed possible witness.** Asked
by old-timers how long AA would last and whether it was still AA, Kurtz answers by pointing at the
individual encounter, one alcoholic seeing in another neither a believer nor an unbeliever but
another alcoholic to be reached out to. The historian with the fullest access to AA's archives
locates the fellowship's persistence in the character of that meeting rather than in the formal
properties of its rules. Chapter Twenty-Five should carry this.

**Wilson on the hazard of the confident individual.** Kurtz quotes a Wilson letter: "Nobody can
cause more grief than a power-driven guy who thinks he has got it straight from God." Wilson adds
that he had had "spells of that very thing" himself.

**Tradition Three's origin as a tolerance problem.** Kurtz describes New York members in 1945
fearing that admitting stigmatized applicants would cost the fellowship its reputation, and
Wilson answering it in the *Grapevine* rather than by rule.

## What it cannot support

It is a talk. Wilson's letters are quoted from memory and without page citations, so anything
taken from them is reported at a remove and should be labelled as such. It carries no data, and
its historical claims should be cited to Kurtz (1991) where that book covers the same ground.
